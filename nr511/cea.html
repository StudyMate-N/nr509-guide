Diagnostic reasoning

The cognitive process clinicians use to gather, weigh, and synthesize subjective and objective data into a working diagnosis. It is hypothesis-driven, iterative, and bounded by pretest probability.

Subjective vs. objective data

Subjective — what the patient tells you

  • Chief complaint, HPI, ROS
  • Pain quality, fatigue, nausea, mood
  • Social, family, surgical history
  • Cannot be measured by you directly

Objective — what you observe or measure

  • Vital signs, physical exam findings
  • Lab values, imaging, ECG
  • Validated screening scores
  • Reproducible by another clinician

How to dissect a question
  1. Read the last line first. Identify what's being asked before you read the stem.
  2. Strip the stem to data points. Demographics, key vitals, one diagnostic clue.
  3. Predict an answer before reading options.
  4. Eliminate two distractors on absolute language ("always", "never").
  5. Choose and move on. Flag, don't dwell.

Night before

  • Light review only — no new content
  • Lay out ID, water, snacks
  • Confirm test center / proctor login
  • Lights out by 10:30

Morning of

  • Protein + complex carb breakfast
  • Caffeine at usual dose, not more
  • Arrive 30 min early
  • Bathroom before block 1
★ Exam tipBetween blocks: stand, stretch, hydrate, look 20 ft away for 20 seconds. Do not review the block you just finished — it's done.
