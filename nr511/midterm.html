HIV Staging & CD4 Thresholds
Week 1 · Always tested

Three stages defined by CD4 count. The exam tests exact threshold numbers and which OIs emerge at each level — memorize the sequence below.

Question 1 of 2
A 34-year-old man with HIV has a CD4 count of 85 cells/μL. He is currently on ART but has a viral load of 2,400 copies/mL. He has never had an opportunistic infection. Which of the following prophylactic medications should he be receiving?
Correct: B — TMP-SMX. CD4 of 85 is below both the PCP threshold (<200) and the Toxoplasma threshold (<100). TMP-SMX covers both simultaneously.
A (MAC prophylaxis with azithromycin): MAC prophylaxis begins at CD4 <50. This patient is at 85. C (fluconazole): Cryptococcal prophylaxis is for CD4 <50. D: CD4 <200 requires PCP prophylaxis regardless of ART status or viral load.
Question 2 of 2
A patient with HIV presents with 3 weeks of headache, fever, and neck stiffness. CD4 count is 38 cells/μL. CSF opening pressure is elevated. India ink stain of the CSF is positive. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic feature of this condition?
Correct: C — NOT TMP-SMX. This is cryptococcal meningitis. TMP-SMX treats PCP and Toxoplasma — NOT Cryptococcus. Cryptococcal meningitis is treated with amphotericin B + flucytosine (induction) → fluconazole (consolidation and maintenance).
All other options are true: A — CD4 <50. B — India ink shows encapsulated yeast. D — caused by Cryptococcus neoformans.
Exam frequency
★★★★★
CD4 thresholds appear on every midterm. The <200 threshold for AIDS and PCP prophylaxis is the most tested single fact in Week 1.
⚠ Exam trap
Confusing Toxo prophylaxis threshold (<100) with PCP threshold (<200). Both use TMP-SMX — the drug is the same but the trigger CD4 count is different.
◆ Clinical pearl
U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable): a patient on effective ART with an undetectable viral load cannot sexually transmit HIV. This changes counseling entirely.
Most missed
MAC prophylaxis threshold. Students confuse <50 (MAC/CMV/Crypto) with <200 (PCP). MAC = <50. PCP = <200. The difference is 4× — not a rounding error.
Bates' / Study guide reference
Hollier Ch. 19 — HIV disease staging and OI prophylaxis thresholds. Chamberlain Week 1 study guide: CD4 table is tested directly.

★★★☆☆
PrEP monitoring requirements appear as a clinical scenario. The 90-90-90 targets appear as a definition question.
⚠ Exam trap
PrEP monitoring interval. The exam tests "every 3 months" — not every 6 months. HIV testing at every visit is mandatory before refilling PrEP.
◆ Clinical pearl

Question 1 of 2
A 24-year-old man presents with 5 days of fever, sore throat, generalized lymphadenopathy, and a diffuse maculopapular rash. He reports an unprotected sexual encounter with a new partner 3 weeks ago. The 70-year-old exceeds the upper age limit (65) and has no identified risk factors — she falls outside the universal screening recommendation.
A: Age 22 = within 15–65 range — screen. B: Age 58 = within 15–65 range — screen. D: All pregnant women are screened at the first prenatal visit regardless of age or risk — USPSTF Grade A recommendation.
Exam frequency
★★★★☆
The window period scenario — negative ELISA + classic acute syndrome — is a confirmed exam question. Students who haven't studied this order Western blot (wrong) or reassure the patient (dangerous).
⚠ Exam trap
Ordering Western blot when ELISA is negative. Western blot is a confirmatory test — it requires a positive ELISA first. It has no role when the ELISA is negative.
◆ Clinical pearl
4th generation ELISA detects both p24 antigen AND antibody — narrowing the window to 18–45 days. 3rd generation tests detected antibody only — window was 3–12 weeks. The current standard is 4th generation.
Most missed
Students see "negative ELISA" and think the patient is HIV-negative. A negative ELISA in the window period means antibodies haven't formed yet — not that infection is absent. The virus is there; the test just can't see it yet.

Herpes Zoster (Shingles)
Week 2 · Highly tested

VZV reactivation from dorsal root ganglia. Prodromal burning pain precedes rash by 3–5 days — treat on clinical suspicion even before the rash appears.

FeatureDetail

ProdromeBurning/tingling/aching pain in the affected dermatome 3–5 days BEFORE rash appears

★ Exam tip — the food rule
Duodenal = food RELIEVES (duodenum is downstream — food buffers acid before it reaches the ulcer). Gastric = food WORSENS (food stimulates parietal cells to secrete more acid onto the damaged stomach wall). The exam gives you a patient and says "pain improves after eating" = duodenal. "Pain is worse after meals" = gastric.
Barrett's esophagus — GERD's most important complication
FeatureDetail
DefinitionMetaplastic replacement of normal squamous esophageal epithelium with intestinal columnar epithelium (goblet cells) — a result of chronic acid injury
CauseChronic GERD — typically 5+ years of untreated or undertreated reflux
SignificancePrecursor to esophageal adenocarcinoma — Barrett's carries 30–40× increased risk of adenocarcinoma
DiagnosisUpper endoscopy (EGD) with biopsy — "salmon-colored" mucosa in the distal esophagus
SurveillanceRegular EGD surveillance is required — interval depends on presence of dysplasia. No dysplasia = every 3–5 years. Low-grade dysplasia = every 6–12 months.
Who to screenMen >50 with chronic GERD symptoms (>5 years) + at least one risk factor: obesity, tobacco use, White race, nocturnal symptoms, hiatal hernia

Question 1 of 2
A 42-year-old man reports 3 months of epigastric pain that wakes him from sleep at 2 AM. He notes that eating a snack at bedtime relieves the pain temporarily. He takes ibuprofen occasionally for back pain. H. pylori breath test returns positive. Which of the following best describes his ulcer type and mechanism?
Correct: B — Duodenal ulcer. Pain relieved by food (eating a snack helps) + nocturnal pain waking at 2 AM (stomach is empty and acid is unbuffered) = classic duodenal ulcer pattern. Food buffers acid in the duodenum downstream from the ulcer. H. pylori is the cause of 70% of duodenal ulcers.
A: Gastric ulcer pain is WORSENED by food — the opposite of this patient's presentation. C: H. pylori causes BOTH gastric and duodenal ulcers — duodenal is actually more common with H. pylori. D: GERD causes heartburn and regurgitation, not food-relieved nocturnal epigastric pain.
Question 2 of 2
A 56-year-old obese man with a 10-year history of GERD reports that his heartburn symptoms are now well-controlled on pantoprazole. An upper endoscopy performed for routine surveillance shows salmon-colored mucosa in the distal esophagus. Biopsy confirms intestinal metaplasia with no dysplasia. Which of the following is the most appropriate management?
Correct: B — Continue PPI and schedule EGD surveillance in 3–5 years. Barrett's esophagus without dysplasia is managed with ongoing acid suppression (PPI) and endoscopic surveillance every 3–5 years. The PPI reduces symptoms and may slow progression but does NOT reverse the metaplastic change.
A: Stopping surveillance because symptoms are controlled is dangerous — Barrett's can progress to adenocarcinoma asymptomatically. C: Esophagectomy is reserved for high-grade dysplasia or early adenocarcinoma — not for non-dysplastic Barrett's. D: PPIs do NOT reverse Barrett's metaplasia — this is the most important false belief to correct.
Exam frequency
★★★★☆
Duodenal vs gastric pain pattern is a confirmed clinical vignette question. Barrett's esophagus is named in the Chamberlain Week 4 study guide — the "PPIs don't reverse Barrett's" trap appears consistently.
⚠ Exam trap
Thinking PPIs reverse Barrett's esophagus. They don't. PPIs control acid and relieve symptoms, but the metaplastic change persists and requires ongoing EGD surveillance regardless of symptom control. Students who think "controlled = cured" miss this.
◆ Clinical pearl
All gastric ulcers require biopsy at endoscopy to rule out gastric adenocarcinoma. Duodenal ulcers do not require routine biopsy — malignant transformation is extremely rare. This asymmetry is tested.
Most missed
Confusing the food-pain relationship. "Duodenal = relieved by food" (mnemonic: D for Downstream — food buffering reaches the duodenum). "Gastric = worsened by food" (food stimulates parietal cell acid secretion directly onto the damaged stomach). Write it out once.
